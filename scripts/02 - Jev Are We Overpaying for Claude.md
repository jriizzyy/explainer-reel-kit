# This new model just exposed why we may be overpaying for Claude

Rewritten using the Explainer Script Framework: familiar analogy → emotional permission → concrete scenario → objection handling → peer-level close.

## Hook

**On-screen text:** "This new model is literally just a multiple-choice exam — for AI agents."

**Spoken:**

"There's a new AI model called Jev. It doesn't chat, it doesn't write code, it doesn't generate a single word of text. And the first time I heard 'decision-only model,' I thought — isn't everything an LLM now? What does that even mean?"

## Spoken lines

1. "Think about it this way. Say you've got an AI agent handling support tickets. All day long it's asking itself: is this spam? Should I retry that failed call? Which tool do I use next? Does a human need to see this? Thousands of tiny decisions — and right now, each one is burning flagship-model money."

2. "Jev just answers the question without the chatbot. You hand it the possible answers up front, and it picks one, with a probability score. It's literally just a multiple-choice exam, but for agents."

3. "It was built by Diogo Almeida — former OpenAI researcher, worked on the RLHF research that taught ChatGPT to follow instructions. One of the people who helped build the chatbot era is now betting the next era isn't chatbots at all."

4. "Now you might think — why not just use a smaller, cheaper chatbot? Technically you could. But a chatbot can still invent an answer you never gave it, or break your output format. Jev's outputs are locked to a schema. It can pick the wrong option — but it can't wander off-script."

5. "TypeSafe, the company behind it, calls Jev a 'System One' model. Scary name, stay with me — it just means fast, instinctive decisions instead of slow word-by-word thinking. Your gut versus writing an essay."

6. "And it's fast — TypeSafe claims up to 50 times faster than a chatbot on these decision calls. But here's the limit: ask Jev to write an essay and it gives you nothing. So this isn't Claude versus Jev. It's Claude for the thinking, Jev for the choosing."

7. "So the uncomfortable question: how many of your Claude calls are just decisions wearing a chatbot costume? The most expensive model isn't always the smartest move. Sometimes it's just the most expensive. Kind of a high-level take — it helps me make sense of it, hopefully it helps you too."

## Cutaways

- Talking head for the hook and the "isn't everything an LLM now?" beat.
- Simple flow graphic: **Input → fixed options → decision + probability scores**.
- On-screen text during the founder beat: "Diogo Almeida — former OpenAI researcher."
- Side-by-side: chatbot's open-ended essay output vs Jev's structured decision output.
- Pause before "decisions wearing a chatbot costume."

## End card

**On-screen text:** "Are AI agents overusing frontier models?"

## Production notes

- Peer, not professor: say the viewer's inner monologue out loud ("scary name, stay with me", "isn't everything an LLM now?").
- One concept per breath; keep runtime tight.
- Keep the schema distinction clear: Jev can't invent new answer types or break format, but its chosen option can still be wrong.
- "50× faster" is TypeSafe's claim for applicable decision calls — attribute it, don't state it as guaranteed end-to-end speed.
- Don't present recreated graphics as real product UI; label mockups.
